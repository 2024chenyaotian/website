Description

These works are perceptually grounded in visual experiences at the microscopic scale, the complex structures of naturalistic forms, and a balanced chromatic order. These experiences, without conscious direction, are intuitively translated into the graphite particles, lines, and amorphous shapes on paper, ultimately forming a series of pencil drawings that exist between abstraction and realism. The inspiration for this series stems from plant morphology, as well as the imagination and observation of internal life structures. Biological imagery such as cells, viruses, and the nervous system are not only objects of rational cognition but also carry my anxiety and reflections on disease and states of disorder. These elements unfold on the canvas with varying emotional densities, some tending toward relief, others appearing stagnant, forming a kind of "neural network" structure that is difficult to accurately describe in language. The works attempt to respond to those internal reactions triggered by the external world in daily experience, yet are often imperceptible. They frequently lack clear causation and are challenging to grasp immediately. Emotion shapes the functioning of the nervous system, and in turn, the neural structure influences the form and trajectory of emotion. In this process, the images detach from the edge of rational order, forming a self-contained and independent visual field that gradually reconstructs my spiritual experience. This series is a record and presentation of this persistent, neurotic characteristic of the everyday state.

这些作品的感知根植于微观尺度的视觉体验、自然形态的复杂结构以及平衡的色彩秩序。这些体验在无意识的引导下，被直觉地转化为纸上的石墨颗粒、线条和无定形形状，最终形成一系列介于抽象与写实之间的铅笔画。该系列的灵感源于植物形态，以及对生命内部结构的想象和观察。细胞、病毒和神经系统等生物意象不仅是理性认知的对象，也承载着我对疾病和紊乱状态的焦虑与思考。这些元素在画布上以不同的情感密度展开，有的趋于舒缓，有的则显得停滞，形成一种难以用语言准确描述的“神经网络”结构。这些作品试图回应日常生活中外部世界引发的内在反应，但这些反应往往难以察觉。它们常常缺乏清晰的因果关系，难以立即理解。情绪塑造着神经系统的运作，反过来，神经结构也影响着情绪的形式和轨迹。在这个过程中，图像脱离了理性秩序的边缘，形成了一个自成一体、独立自主的视觉领域，并逐渐重构了我的精神体验。本系列作品记录并呈现了这种持续存在的、神经质的日常状态特征。
