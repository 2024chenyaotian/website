Work Details

  • Medium: Nüshu, soybean sprout, white tulip petals, outer tepal of Alstroemeria, white chrysanthemum petals, green carnation inflorescence, avocado seed coat, Cyperus inflorescence, gold and silver synthetic thread.
  • Year: 2025

Description

This work blends research on Nüshu, a secret Chinese women's script, with botany as an alternative knowledge system. I associated this secretive, 19th-century language not with power, but with tenderness, care, and kinship for the natural world. The resulting, extremely light sculptures are designed to move with a single breath. Nüshu, invented by women denied formal education, historically fostered sisterhood through songs, embroidery, and hidden writings. Honoring this history, and my family's tradition of needlework, I created these sculptures by weaving fibers with natural materials and embroidering coded Nüshu vocabularies onto the petals or leaves. Though not immediately legible, their meanings are translated in the titles, chosen for their connection to personal memory, family, love, and trauma. The weaving, sewing, and embroidery serve not as mere decoration, but as an active intention to mend and care. The natural materials were foraged in New York City, collected from sidewalks, gutters, parks, and local markets. This process allowed me to engage with my surroundings on a different time-scale, re-tuning myself to the discarded and overlooked elements of the urban environment. These "forage walks" also helped me develop a material archive connecting botanical life with language systems.

这项作品融合了对女书（一种神秘的中国女性文字）的研究和植物学——一种另类的知识体系。我将这种神秘的19世纪语言与温柔、关怀以及对自然界的亲缘关系联系起来，而非权力。最终创作出的极其轻盈的雕塑，只需一口气即可移动。女书由被剥夺了正规教育的女性创造，历史上曾通过歌曲、刺绣和隐秘的文字来维系姐妹情谊。为了致敬这段历史以及我家族的针线活传统，我将纤维与天然材料编织在一起，并在花瓣或叶片上绣上编码的女书词汇，创作了这些雕塑。虽然这些词汇乍看之下难以辨认，但它们的含义已在作品的标题中得到诠释，这些标题的选择都与个人记忆、家庭、爱和创伤息息相关。编织、缝纫和刺绣并非仅仅是装饰，而是体现了一种积极的修补和关怀的意图。这些天然材料均取自纽约市，包括人行道、排水沟、公园和当地市场。这个过程让我得以用不同的时间尺度与周围环境互动，重新关注那些被遗弃和忽视的城市环境元素。这些“觅食漫步”也帮助我建立了一个将植物生命与语言系统联系起来的物质档案。
